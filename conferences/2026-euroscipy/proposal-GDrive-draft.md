**Title:** How a sustainability project (re)-connected a foundational library community with downstream developers: Successes, failures, surprises, and moving forward, together

**Abstract:** (200 to 1500 characters)

Foundational libraries provide key functionality and resources that many developers and users depend on. The teams maintaining these libraries must make choices in where to spend their limited resources, but gaps in awareness plague the effectiveness of building and sustaining communities. Napari—a foundational library for interactive, multi-dimensional image and data viewing built on the scientific Python stack—is easily extensible through an ecosystem of over 580 community-developed plugins. 

These plugins are relied on by users to access file I/O, interactive analysis, the newest deep learning models, quantification, and more, by folks ranging from complete novices to cutting-edge developers in diverse disciplines including biology, archaeology, physics, neuroscience, and astronomy. After an initial burst of community organization and centralized support, napari and its plugin ecosystem started to experience growing pains that have hampered the success of napari and plugin developers and ultimately decreased the experience of users everywhere. 

In order to improve the experience for all, napari started the community **plugin sustainability initiative** and has (re)-connected developers and users of all stages. This talk will discuss the community-created plans to improve discussion, documentation, and tooling for napari and plugin development aiming to improve sustainability of individual projects and the health of the whole ecosystem.
