Over the past few months of my time closer to the napari community, we have identified a need and desire for the plugin ecosystem to improve--from community resources, to infrastructure, to maintenance, and more. Overall, the goal of this new plugin sustainability initiative  is to strengthen the whole community and improve the napari plugin ecosystem so that *everyone* benefits. I hope you'll join me in this new venture! **Every** person in the napari community has something of value to bring because we want to support both long-time and brand new developers and users. :handshake: Please take less than a minute to fill out this [Interest Form](https://forms.gle/iwX6P4tuooHK5aKH9)! :pray: 

The startup of this plugin sustainability working group is supported by a six month fellowship from [URSSI](https://urssi.us/blog/2025/09/23/urssi-welcomes-second-cohort-of-early-career-fellows/). You can read the [original fellowship proposal](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1tIdGOUB8Lw8FIirAAE8dh5QJhNuXnJcZyBM6Q_fOmJc/edit?usp=sharing) to see proposed actions, goals, timeline, and deliverables. However, most importantly, improving the napari and plugin ecosystem foremost requires the community because this is the only way to ensure sustainability, so the initial proposal is only meant as ideas to get us started and guide prioritization of efforts. Current actionable goals include:

1. Build a network to centralize community resources (time, effort, stories) so that proactive feedback and collaborative development exists between plugin authors, users, and the core napari project.
2. Create an initiative that encourages best practices and highlights community work. The current idea is a community plugin review modeled after, or in conjunction with, [pyOpenSci peer review](https://www.pyopensci.org/about-peer-review/) and [JOSS](https://joss.theoj.org/). 
3. Improve the plugin development infrastructure by updating the [napari-plugin-template](https://github.com/napari/napari-plugin-template) to modern best practices, improve documentation and tutorials. This may include changes to npe2 to support developers.
4. Create tooling to evaluate plugin health and support sustainable maintenance. This tooling could be used in conjunction with peer review, but stands on its own as a way to evaluate the status of the ecosystem now, at the end of the fellowship, and in the future. May become part of the [weather-report](https://napari.org/weather-report/).

The current plan will be to use this Zulip and Github discussions for asynchronous discussions and *once* weekly Zoom meetings that mirror the "Pacific" and "Atlantic" community meeting times on alternate weeks ([see the calendar](https://napari.org/stable/community/meeting_schedule.html) :calendar: ).  We can discuss moving the meetings to a time and frequency to serve the community as needed. The first meeting is tentatively <time:2025-10-09T20:00:00-05:00>.

I will be pinging many active developers of plugins to try to garner more attention, but if you are not contacted please know that you are encouraged to participate (I'd say perhaps more so).
